# Hoppers, Live Bottoms, Silos, Chutes

## Hoppers and live bottoms
- Bio-LIVEBOTTOM calculator + David Diller's LiveBottom Calculation Procedure (Sep 2026) in Bio-HANDLING Tools.
- Standard roof plate 1/4" (meets a typical 100 psf roof load with suitable C-channel). Standard live-bottom connection L-angle 3/8". [JMS std]
- A 250 psf roof live load was challenged as unreasonable; the customer accepted 100 psf. [Lesson]
- Twin-screw live bottoms share one outlet slot, so hopper outlet width B spans both screws. [JMS std]
- Welds on outsourced hoppers:
  - Vertical C-channel structural attachments carrying hopper load need 100% continuous welds. Horizontal shell stiffeners may be stitch welded.
  - Weld sizing and callouts are Engineering's responsibility. Give the fabricator the RISA report.
- Finish:
  - SS hoppers are blasted (BU standard, for appearance); live bottoms are not.
  - Welds may need pickling/passivation per spec to prevent rust blooms.
- Hydro testing (24057): two tests. First full fill with blanking plates, then reduced fill with slide gates installed. Needs custom CS painted test blinds, ~5–7 business days per live bottom. Build it into the schedule early. [Lesson]
- Lifting provisions on major weldments (~1,000 lb+), with center of mass considered. [JMS std]
- Large-hopper tolerance: add leg-height measurement and tolerance callouts (25034-HP-M100). [Lesson]

## Load cells
- Kistler Morse:
  - Live hanging-weight auto-calibration needs ≥25% of max capacity. Otherwise calibrate manually, then refine by removing a weighed amount and adjusting scale-factor counts.
  - A conveyor bridging two hoppers can be tared out if the bins are calibrated with ≥25% of rated load.
  - Support a bridge entirely from one hopper (beam + knee brace) with ~1/2" gaps at the other. OSHA treats an opening >2" as a hole to guard.
- Load-cell base plates need ~1/16" horizontal alignment, which is hard with cast-in anchors.
- Cast-in anchor length must cover embed + sleeve/stretch + grout + base plate + nut. 12" was too short on 23058. [Lesson]

## Silos
- Loadout sequence (22027 Beloit): start chute → rotary valve → vibrator after ~1 min. Stop the vibrator first and the rotary valve last (~10 s delay).
- Thermocouples:
  - They are sensitive to terminations; false highs can trip the nitrogen/fire response. Ask for historical trending.
  - Seal top hanger penetrations, or water runs down the support wire. [Lesson]
- Explosion panels must clear stairs (OSHA 7.5 ft minimum headroom).
- Dust collection: historically 500 SCFM per pick point, usually 2 points (silo vent + retractable chute) → 1,000 SCFM. Duct length and size matter; don't upsize without cause. [JMS std / Judgment]
- Motors inside a silo shell may need Class I Div 1, while the EoR accepted "CIID2" for dust collector motors on 25034 (read as Class II Div 2; confirm on each job, as classification is EoR-dependent).

## Chutes
- Plate seams split on a 45° line from the corner. [JMS std]
- Flexible chutes:
  - minimum heights ~4" (US Bellows, Elasto-Valve), 6" (PROCO); no supplier could make a 2.75" chute rated 5 psi
  - neoprene lead time ~12–14 wk
  - if clearance is too tight, use a solid SS chute with the adjacent equipment adjusted (±3/8" adjustability) [Lesson 25034]
- Flag contradictory spec language (e.g., "flexible, rubber, heavy-duty") to the customer.
